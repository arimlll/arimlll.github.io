This article sets forth the unreasonable aspects identified during the study of the online "Shanghai College Student Safety Education" course and presents suggestions regarding the course structure and the policies of relevant educational authorities.

1. **Outdated Content and Mechanistic Examination**: The course structure is mechanical and fails to meaningfully cultivate practical safety awareness among university students. It demands disproportionate time and energy, carrying strong overtones of bureaucratic formalism.

2. **Mandatory Enforcement Without Sufficient Consultation**: The course was pushed forcefully without adequate consultation or soliciting substantive feedback from students and universities. This has sparked widespread resentment, contradicting student-centered pedagogy and undermining actual learning outcomes.

3. **Platform Exclusivity Tied to a Commercial App**: The course exclusively designated "Chaoxing Xuexitong" (学习通) as the sole platform, requiring students to download its application or access its web portal for learning and examinations. This raises legitimate concerns regarding the binding of administrative mandates to specific commercial platforms.

4. **Curricular Duplication with Existing Campus Courses**: Universities have already implemented offline courses on national security fundamentals, whose syllabus heavily overlaps with this online course, rendering repeated study of minimal incremental value.

While safety education in higher education institutions is unquestionably necessary, it must reject formalism, coercive mandates, and commercial platform binding. Safety education should genuinely center on students and effectiveness rather than degenerating into superficial compliance projects. Relevant departments are encouraged to heed student feedback, optimize curricula, and truly enhance the safety competencies of young people.

*The Shanghai 12345 civic hotline platform has accepted the suggestions submitted in this article.*
